\section{Limitations and Open Directions}\label{sec:open}

\paragraph{Finite-memory scope.}
The cube-root law of \cref{prop:coherence_delay} is exact for the
finite-memory averaging class analyzed here, and \cref{prop:window_minimax}
extends the lower-bound side to arbitrary measurable estimators that only see
the last $m$ samples of a $\zeta$-Lipschitz drift path. The cube-root exponent
therefore survives beyond linear averaging in the critical-window regime,
but this still falls short of a universal minimax result over arbitrary
recursive or extrapolative adaptive estimators.

\paragraph{Geometry and observability.}
The present formulation uses $W_2$ and its debiased Sinkhorn approximation to
measure model drift. Extending the same trade-off cleanly to other geometries,
such as $f$-divergences or kernel MMD, remains open. Likewise,
$CI^E$ (written formally as $\ScoreE(\IS,t)$ when needed) requires an observable
proxy for intervention effort, and the preferred proxy is domain-dependent.
This is especially relevant in performative or feedback-loop settings, where
intervention and exposure are partially confounded
\citep{PerdomoEtAl2020,HardtMendlerDunner2023,TaoriHashimoto2023,KrauthWangJordan2022}.

\paragraph{Benchmark dependence.}
The passive benchmarks use Page-Hinkley events as the operational reference for
lead time, and different failure definitions would change the resulting alarm
statistics. On active and logged benchmarks, the usefulness of
CI$^E$ still depends on calibration choices such as the effort penalty
$\lambda$, the warning threshold, and the local drift estimator
$\hat\zeta_t$.

\paragraph{Effort proxies and $\sigma_A$ surrogates.}
The practical hierarchy of effort proxies is strongest in simulation,
weaker in direct observation, and weakest in logged recommendation data.
Likewise, $\sigma_A$ is exact only in settings with replicated or fully
specified action channels. In the Gaussian tracker it is constructive, in the
particle tracker it is a controlled surrogate based on actuation gap, and in
KuaiRand it is a normalized exposure-entropy proxy rather than a direct
estimate of $e^{-H(\Act\mid\Pot)}$. The logged benchmark should therefore be
read as operational evidence for effort-aware diagnostics, not as full causal
identification of intervention dynamics. A stronger causal claim would require
either replica-policy or shadow-policy logs, or an external human-grounded
outcome surface against which masking could be falsified rather than inferred
from the benchmark's own internal signals. More richly logged recommender traces
that preserve full slate or action-channel realizations would also strengthen
the estimand itself: in that regime $\sigma_A$ could move from a proxy-level
surrogate toward the identified tier of the hierarchy in \cref{def:tci_estimable}.

\paragraph{Scaling beyond present validation.}
The Gaussian and particle trackers validate the theory in controlled Gaussian and non-Gaussian
settings, and the real-data benchmarks show that the diagnostics remain useful
outside the synthetic environment. High-dimensional state spaces, richer action
channels, and direct measurements of intervention effort are the next natural
tests of the framework. A particularly sharp next benchmark is RLHF-style
post-training. That setting instantiates all three roles unusually cleanly: the
current policy plays the role of $\Pot$, sampled completions or actions play the
role of $\Act$, and the policy update rule plays the role of $\Conv$.
Policy-reference KL is already logged as an intervention burden in standard
RLHF pipelines \citep{StiennonEtAl2020,BaiEtAl2022}, making it a much cleaner
effort proxy than the exposure surrogates available in logged recommendation
data. At the same time, reward-model or proxy-reward improvement can be compared
against held-out or human-grounded quality, creating an external masking surface
that the present KuaiRand benchmark does not have. In the language of this
paper, RLHF is therefore a natural next test bed for the masking gap
$M_t = CI - CI^E$ (formally, $\Score(\IS,t) - \ScoreE(\IS,t)$): the system may maintain high proxy
coherence while paying increasing KL cost and drifting away from the external
target. Practical simulation frameworks such as AlpacaFarm \citep{DuboisEtAl2024}
make that direction especially plausible without requiring full-scale LLM
training in the loop.

\paragraph{Opaque adaptation maps.}
For black-box $\Conv_t$, the present manuscript provides only the template
requirement that an ISS-Lyapunov functional $V_\Phi$ exist. A CEGIS-style
recovery route is not implemented or validated in
this repository, so diagnosing $\sigma_\Phi$ for opaque adaptation subsystems
remains an open engineering problem.

\paragraph{Computational profile.}
The Sinkhorn runtime study is a prototype-level NumPy benchmark on Gaussian
clouds. It is useful for separating statistical accuracy from wall-clock cost,
but it does not claim a production implementation ceiling. The current sweep
reaches $d=256$ and $\varepsilon=0.02$, and the main practical message is that
fixed regularization is not portable across geometry: larger $\varepsilon$ is
consistently cheaper and more accurate on this grid, while the residual bias and
iteration count still depend on dimension. The current calibration is therefore
operational rather than asymptotically exact: it is meant to compare tracked
drifted clouds at a chosen operating point, not to claim a null-optimal threshold
rule. Recent Sinkhorn limit theorems make that split explicit, with distinct
alternative-regime and null fluctuation orders \citep{GoldfeldEtAl2022}.

\paragraph{Missing adaptive baselines.}
The passive comparisons emphasize ADWIN, Page-Hinkley, KSWIN, and the NoDrift
sanity baseline, and now also include CUSUM, forgetting-factor recursive least
squares, scalar Kalman predictors, and a kernel MMD two-sample test
(Gaussian RBF, median-heuristic bandwidth) on the real-world streams. That does
not exhaust the baseline space: multivariate MMD detectors, representation-space
shift tests, and additional online adaptive learners such as explicit $H_\infty$
filters remain future work, and the present calibration should still be read as
benchmark-specific rather than universal.

\section{Limitations and Further Directions}\label{sec:limitations_open_problems}

The theorem-level results comprise the temporal-validity
horizon, the induced useful-memory region, the exact uniform-window staleness
bound, a tractable one-dimensional root-$n$ proof model, a structural Gaussian
lower bound at the exponent level, a Gaussian location benchmark, and
horizon-inference coverage and regret under lag-geometry input. Other results
concern narrower transfer, operational, or empirical statements.

\paragraph{Closed theory.}
The fully proved finite-sample instantiation is the one-dimensional root-$n$
regime under bounded support and fixed span. The structural lower bound is
subclass based, and the exact Gaussian constants are internal to the available
proof scheme rather than class-tight across broader deterministic H\"older path
classes. Finite-family projection transfer is theorem-level under uniform
projected control, but richer dominated families and raw multivariate Wasserstein
lie beyond the proved range.

\paragraph{Embedded Sinkhorn.}
For fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn, the compact-support one-dimensional pointwise
and bandwise inheritance results are explicit, and bounded-cardinality finite
discrete embedded classes satisfy the same moderate-band root-$n$ law under a
uniform diameter bound and a positive mass floor. The strongest support-growth
evidence is concentrated on moderate bands bounded away from zero, especially on
the calibrated $(8,2)$ and $(12,2)$ regimes. On the calibrated discrete $k=2$
support grid, the centered $S^2$ self-coupling gap, the exact-target null
slopes, the exact $\ell_2$ fluctuation law, and the finite-support root-$n$
fluctuation proxy are all favorable. The support-growth theorem is organized by
a covariance-weighted trace bound and a local block-curvature bound for the null
Sinkhorn map on that family, and Taylor expansion yields the quadratic null
control. Current numerical Hessian probes indicate anisotropic curvature, with
collective directions scaling approximately linearly in the support-growth
parameter. The trace proxy also grows linearly in the support-growth parameter
at about $0.34$ per block, while the covariance-weighted proxy stays small,
around $0.002$ to $0.004$. The exact row-kernel decomposition reduces the
curvature analysis to one odd channel and one two-dimensional even channel.

\paragraph{Empirical scope.}
The experiments target structural signatures: U-curves, roughness scaling,
real-stream retention, and validity-detection separation. They do not identify
detector-uniform delay laws or competitive end-to-end performance.

\paragraph{Further directions.}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{High-dimensional horizon geometry.}
  Identify which transport geometries in higher dimension preserve a nondegenerate
  temporal-validity horizon, especially under intrinsic-dimension adaptation,
  smoothed transport, and projection-robust formulations.

  \item \textbf{Sequential validity versus detection delay.}
  Quantify the structural gap between the validity horizon and the time-uniform
  detection horizon under false-alarm control, and determine how that gap scales
  across detector classes.

  \item \textbf{Adaptive horizon inference.}
  Develop stable estimators of the roughness and amplitude parameters that drive
  the horizon law, with explicit guarantees for memory localization under lag
  noise.

  \item \textbf{Support-changing embedded Sinkhorn.}
  Extend the calibrated embedded Sinkhorn theorem to classes with changing support
  and beyond the fixed support-growth construction.
\end{enumerate}

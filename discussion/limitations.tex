\section{Limitations and Open Problems}\label{sec:limitations_open_problems}

The proved results cover the temporal-validity horizon, its useful-memory
geometry, the one-dimensional proof anchor, the Gaussian lower and benchmark
results, the embedded Sinkhorn theorems, and horizon-inference guarantees.

The remaining open problems are the following.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Class-tight lower theory beyond the Gaussian benchmark.}
  The abstract lower proposition establishes that the temporal-validity horizon
  is structural by means of a Gaussian two-point subclass, and the Gaussian
  location benchmark closes the corresponding minimax rate on the deterministic
  H\"older class. What remains open is class-tight lower theory for broader
  drifting classes beyond that benchmark model.

  \item \textbf{Transfer beyond the proved projected range.}
  Finite-family projection transfer is proved under uniform projected control,
  and the effective-exponent corollary shows how any geometry with a
  proved finite-sample exponent inherits a horizon scale. What remains unproved
  is an analogous theorem for richer dominated families and for raw
  multivariate Wasserstein, where the effective exponent can become too small
  for practical memory selection.

  \item \textbf{Support-changing embedded Sinkhorn classes.}
  For fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn, the compact-support one-dimensional
  pointwise and bandwise inheritance results are explicit, bounded-cardinality
  finite discrete embedded classes satisfy the same moderate-band root-$n$ law,
  and the calibrated support-growth family is closed on the discrete $k=2$
  grid. The open extension is support change beyond that fixed
  support-growth construction.

  \item \textbf{Online validity control under streaming noise.}
  Horizon inference closes plug-in asymptotics, identifiability, deterministic
  stability, and quadratic regret under the lag-geometry input model. The open
  operational problem is to obtain stable online estimation and time-uniform
  memory localization guarantees under streaming noise. The experiments target
  structural signatures rather than detector-uniform delay laws or competitive
  end-to-end performance, so sequential validity versus detection remains open
  at the level of general theory.
\end{enumerate}

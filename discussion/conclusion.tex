\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Under drift, memory is not only a resource; it is also a source of staleness.
Under feedback, good observed fit is not yet evidence that the model is current,
because intervention can keep the world near a stale estimate. Taken together,
those two facts point to two claims: a finite-memory tracking floor, and an
intervention-aware diagnostic for when apparent coherence is being purchased
rather than earned.

The tracking result is a sharp coherence-delay limit for the finite-memory
averaging class. When total error is written as a sum of estimation variance and
drift-induced staleness, optimizing over memory yields the cube-root law
$\mathcal{E}_{\min} = \tfrac{3}{2}C_K^{2/3}\zeta^{1/3}$ for the estimator class
analyzed here. The broader lower-bound argument then shows that the same
exponent is not merely a sliding-window artifact: it reappears for arbitrary
estimators restricted to the last $m$ samples on a $\zeta$-Lipschitz drift
class in the critical-window regime. The practical lesson is blunt: more data
can still make a tracker worse if the world moves faster than the memory can
stay current.

The diagnostic result addresses a specific failure mode of action-coupled
systems: apparent coherence maintained by forcing rather than by adaptation. The
decomposition into representation, actuation, and adaptation roles
$(\Pot,\Act,\Conv)$ isolates that regime from ordinary degradation, and the
Effort-Adjusted Coherence Index ($CI^E$) is built for exactly that case. The
empirical results support the distinction. The Gaussian tracker recovers the
expected U-curve and the $\zeta^{1/3}$ scaling law for both sliding windows and
EWMA. The particle benchmark shows that feedback masking can keep the standard
score high even when the model is stale. On passive streams, generic
changepoint detectors such as ADWIN remain the stronger default alarms. On
active and logged benchmarks, however, the effort-aware score becomes the right
diagnostic because the failure mode is no longer passive drift alone. On
KuaiRand, the gain of $CI^E$ over $CI$ is statistically stable under paired
bootstrap resampling, but the logged benchmark remains proxy-level evidence
rather than causal identification of masking; within that proxy regime, KL is
also the strongest effort proxy among the logged alternatives tested here. The
practical tuning rule is correspondingly simple: set $E_0$ to the healthy
effort scale, keep $\lambda$ in the empirically stable $2$--$3$ range, and use
the KL proxy when only logged exposure summaries are available. Those settings
are not universal optima; they are the operating point supported by the current
logged benchmark. The most natural next test of the same idea is RLHF-style
post-training, where policy KL already measures intervention burden and
proxy-reward divergence provides a sharper external masking surface.

The broader claim is modest, but useful. Beyond the specific results, this
framework points toward finite-memory design rules under drift,
intrinsic-dimension-aware OT estimation, and principled diagnostics for
performative or action-coupled settings. Adaptive systems should not be judged
only by how well they fit what they currently see. They should also be judged by
how much temporal staleness they can tolerate and by how much intervention is
required to preserve the appearance of coherence. Within the scope studied here,
the cube-root law explains the tracking floor, and $CI^E$ explains why low
observed error can still be the wrong comfort signal under feedback.

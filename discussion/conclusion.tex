\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Tracking under drift with finite
effective memory obeys a sharp limit. When total error is written as a sum of estimation variance
and drift-induced staleness, optimizing over memory yields the cube-root law
$\mathcal{E}_{\min} = \tfrac{3}{2}C_K^{2/3}\zeta^{1/3}$ for the estimator class
analyzed here. In that sense, tracking under drift is constrained by the same
object that makes online compression useful: memory. More memory reduces noise,
but it also increases the age of the compressed model.

Action-coupled settings admit a minimal decomposition into
representation, actuation, and adaptation roles $(\Pot,\Act,\Conv)$. In this
setting the decomposition separates three experimentally
meaningful failure modes and supports bottleneck-sensitive diagnostics. The score
measures ordinary coherence, while $\ScoreE$ corrects the specific regime
in which apparent coherence is maintained by intervention rather than by model
adaptation.

The empirical results support this structure. The Gaussian tracker recovers the
predicted U-curve and the $\zeta^{1/3}$ scaling law in the controlled setting.
The particle tracker shows that coercive masking can keep the standard score
high even when
the underlying model is stale. On passive benchmarks, generic changepoint
detectors such as ADWIN remain stronger default alarms. On the logged
KuaiRand benchmark, however, the effort-corrected score detects a mode of
feedback-induced degradation that the generic baselines do not capture under the
same calibration.

Finite-memory tracking under drift obeys a structural coherence-delay trade-off.
Closed-loop systems can hide model failure through feedback, and effort-aware
diagnostics can surface that regime.
How far this decomposition extends beyond the settings studied here remains
open.

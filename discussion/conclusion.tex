\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Validity under drift is constrained by the validity of the information state
used at the present time. Retention reduces finite-sample noise and ages the
retained state simultaneously, so validity has a finite horizon.

In the deterministic H"older--Wasserstein constitutive model, the horizon law
\[
  \mathbb E\,d\!\left(\widehat P_t^{(n)},P_t\right)
  \le C_K n^{-a} + C_S\zeta n^H,
\]
and its optimizer
\[
  n^*(a,H) \asymp (C_K/\zeta)^{1/(a+H)}
\]
summarize that balance. This law induces a useful-memory region around the
optimal scale.

A structural Gaussian lower bound and a Gaussian location benchmark identify the
horizon as a property of drift geometry rather than of a particular estimator.
A tractable one-dimensional proof anchor and projection-based transfer establish
the same mechanism beyond the benchmark class.

The empirical signatures align with the theory: U-curves,
roughness-dependent horizon scaling, a real-stream finite-memory pattern, a
positive lag between validity loss and detector alarm, a regime-dependent policy
frontier under actuation cost, and a conformal calibration window with the same
finite-memory geometry. Horizon inference from lag geometry captures the
observability side: validity can be structurally defined while remaining hard to
estimate from data alone.

Inferential persistence therefore has a measurable profile with a critical scale
under flow. The central object is the validity of the information state itself.
Memory choice, calibration-window choice, and sequential control are all
operational readouts of that same object.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Inferential structures under drift combine finite-sample gain with temporal
aging. The resulting trade-off gives retained statistical structure a
temporal-validity profile with a finite horizon.

The horizon law
\[
  \mathbb E\,d\!\left(\widehat P_t^{(n)},P_t\right)
  \le C_K n^{-a} + C_S\zeta n^H,
\]
and its optimizer
\[
  n^*(a,H) \asymp (C_K/\zeta)^{1/(a+H)}
\]
summarize that balance. This law induces a useful-memory region around the
optimal scale.

Three proved cases establish the same temporal-validity law: a tractable
one-dimensional root-$n$ proof anchor, bounded-cardinality finite-discrete
embedded Sinkhorn, and a calibrated support-growth embedded Sinkhorn family. A
structural Gaussian lower bound and a Gaussian location benchmark identify the
horizon as a property of drift geometry rather than of a particular estimator.

The empirical signatures align with the theory: finite-memory U-curves,
roughness-dependent horizon scaling, a real-stream finite-memory pattern, and a
positive lag between validity loss and detector alarm. Validity observability
captures the operational side: a temporal-validity horizon can be structurally
defined while remaining hard to estimate from lag geometry alone.

Inferential persistence has a measurable profile with a critical persistence
scale under drift.

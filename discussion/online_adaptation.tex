\section{Operational Use of Validity}\label{sec:online_adaptation}

The validity horizon becomes operational once memory is treated as a
controlled state. The same finite-sample versus staleness balance that defines
the horizon also determines when an online controller should update, defer, or
hold its memory scale. The resulting problem is not to maximize memory or to
react as soon as possible, but to keep the information state inside its useful
band at acceptable cost.

The figure above summarizes the online object directly. Aggregating lagged
discrepancies improves roughness estimation, the structural controller reduces
tracking error relative to the raw plug-in rule, and the preferred scale moves
with the drift phase rather than remaining fixed. The online use of temporal
validity is therefore a combined inference-and-control problem.

Across the five tested profile families, the structural controller consistently
improves on the raw plug-in rule and beats the adaptive EWMA and adaptive
Kalman baselines on the majority of runs. The gains are strongest on the rough
and alternating profiles, where the online memory state must move with drift
while still resisting noise. This is the operational interpretation of the
validity horizon: a memory scale is useful only while its retained
information remains temporally valid, and the controller must pay cost to keep
it there.

The controller evidence is therefore not a separate story from the horizon law.
It is the sequential readout of the same object. Horizon inference estimates the
usable band; online control decides whether to deploy it, hold it, or update it
under cost.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Tracking under non-stationarity.}
This work sits at the intersection of three literatures that are usually kept
apart: concept drift detection, finite-memory tracking, and action-coupled
prediction. In the concept-drift literature, classical detectors such as ADWIN,
DDM, and Page-Hinkley identify changepoints or sustained mean shifts in a
monitored signal \citep{GamaSurvey2014,BifetGavalda2007,Gama2004,Harries1999}.
Those methods remain the right passive baselines, and the empirical ordering
here leaves that conclusion intact: on passive streams they are still the
stronger default alarms. The role of the Coherence Index (CI) is narrower. It
is a structural diagnostic tied to model fidelity, actuation reproducibility,
and update quality, not a universal replacement for generic changepoint
detection.

The second neighborhood is nonstationary optimization, adaptive filtering, and
online least-squares. There the same cube-root tension between variation and
memory already appears, but in Euclidean loss, bandit, dynamic-regret, or
parameter-tracking settings rather than in a distributional
\(W_2\)/Sinkhorn geometry
\citep{BesbesGurZeevi2015,CheungSimchiLeviZhu2019,YuanLamperski2020,MazzettoUpfal2023}.
The $1/3$ exponent itself is therefore not presented as wholly new. The more
specific claim is that, in this setting, the same balance can be written as an
explicit finite-memory error law for causal averaging estimators under
$W_2$-Lipschitz drift, carried through to an operational selector for window
size or forgetting factor, and matched by a minimax obstruction for arbitrary
window-restricted estimators on the same drift class. Classical smoothers such
as EWMA, forgetting-factor RLS, and Kalman filtering belong to the same broad
family of variance-versus-responsiveness trade-offs, but they are usually
studied as estimators rather than as components of a closed-loop diagnostic
argument. Disturbance-observer work on KF-DOB makes a closely related
bias-variance trade-off explicit, but in a model-mismatch setting rather than a
drift-controlled memory law \citep{LiEtAl2024}. The EWMA results matter here
because they show that the cube-root law is a finite-memory phenomenon rather
than a sliding-window artifact. What is added is the combination of that law
with a closed-loop diagnostic and a practical memory selector.

\paragraph{Transport-based diagnostics.}
WATCH uses Wasserstein geometry to detect distribution shift in feature space
\citep{faber2022watch,FaberEtAl2022LIFEWATCH}. The relation is close, but the
role of transport is different. There, Wasserstein distance is the detector.
Here, $W_2$ and its debiased Sinkhorn surrogate are built into the tracking
model itself: they quantify representation error, induce the lag--variance
trade-off, and anchor the coherence diagnostics. The point is not simply that
OT is a good shift statistic, but that in this setting it belongs to the
geometry of what it means for a finite-memory tracker to stay current. On the
statistical side, the finite-sample convergence rates of
empirical Wasserstein distances depend on both the sample size and the intrinsic
dimension of the support \citep{NilesWeedBach2019}; these rates inform the
constant $C_K$ in our bounds and suggest that practitioners tuning
$\varepsilon$ in higher dimensions should consult dimension-aware calibration
rather than using a fixed regularization schedule. Recent limit theorems for the
Sinkhorn divergence also clarify the calibration split that matters here:
bootstrap-friendly alternative-regime behavior and a different null limit order
for threshold setting \citep{GoldfeldEtAl2022}. In the Gaussian case, exact
entropic OT structure is even available in closed form, which helps explain why
the prototype runtime study behaves cleanly on Gaussian clouds even when the
constants still move with geometry \citep{AkyildizEtAl2024}.

\paragraph{Kernel-based shift detection.}
Kernel two-sample tests based on the Maximum Mean Discrepancy
(MMD) \citep{GrettonEtAl2012} provide a complementary nonparametric approach
to distribution shift detection, and several streaming drift detectors build on
them. The MMD and $W_2$ address related but distinct aspects of distributional
change: MMD measures discrepancy in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space and is
straightforward to threshold for hypothesis testing, while $W_2$ inherits the
metric geometry of the signal space and connects directly to the lag--variance
trade-off studied here. We now include an MMD-based detector on the real-world
passive streams, so direct empirical comparison is available there; however, the
CI/CI$^E$ diagnostics operate at a different level than a shift alarm, since
they score the structural bottleneck of a three-role system rather than testing
whether two windows are drawn from the same distribution.

\paragraph{Filtering and closed-loop control.}
The representation--action--adaptation decomposition is motivated by standard
state-estimation and control logic, not by a claim that every adaptive system
must have three roles. ISS theory supplies the stability template
\citep{sontag1989,dashkovskiy2010,MironchenkoP2020}, while classical filtering
provides the concrete Gaussian realization used here
\citep{Jazwinski1970}. Once actions affect
future observations, state update must solve a credit-assignment problem rather
than a passive filtering problem.

\paragraph{Belief update semantics.}
The distinction between revision and update in dynamic worlds
\citep{KatsunaMendelzon1992} gives a formal language for the same issue.
\citet{Bonanno2024} makes explicit that action-conditioned belief update must
distinguish exogenous change from state changes caused by the agent itself. That
observation matters here because it justifies the presence of the action
argument in the closed-loop update.

\paragraph{Algorithmic regulation and compression.}
\citet{Ruffini2026} interprets good regulation through algorithmic compression
of realized trajectories. The present framework adds a temporal constraint
absent from that picture: even a useful compressed model becomes stale under
drift. The cube-root law therefore complements the algorithmic-regulation
viewpoint by quantifying how finite memory limits the duration for which
compression remains valid in a changing environment.

\paragraph{Action-coupled prediction.}
The coercive masking regime is related to broader work on systems that change
the data-generating process they seek to model. The claim is narrower than a
general performative-prediction analysis: the focus here is a diagnostic
failure mode in which low observed error can be sustained by intervention, along
with an effort-aware correction for that case. This connects most directly
to performative prediction and feedback-loop analyses in recommendation and
dataset construction, including
\citep{PerdomoEtAl2020,HardtMendlerDunner2023,FarinaPerdomo2026,TaoriHashimoto2023,PaganEtAl2023,KrauthWangJordan2022,EilatRosenfeld2023}.
The main distinction is that the aim is not to solve the full causal problem of
performative feedback or recommendation confounding, but to diagnose when
intervention effort masks stale models in active or logged systems. Strategic
classification \citep{HardtMPW2016} studies a closely
related phenomenon from the opposite side: agents modify their features to
game a deployed classifier, creating a feedback loop between the model and the
data it evaluates. Our coercive masking regime is the system-side analogue:
the system modifies the environment rather than the agents modifying
themselves. CI$^E$ does not solve the full strategic or performative problem;
it provides a diagnostic for when low observed error should stop counting as
evidence of good adaptation.
Performative stability asks where retraining dynamics settle under the
distribution induced by their own predictions \citep{PerdomoEtAl2020}; recent
online-learning results show that broad no-regret procedures can converge to
mixed performatively stable equilibria even without strong structural
restrictions \citep{FarinaPerdomo2026}. Coercive masking asks a complementary
question. A system may remain near such an apparent equilibrium while paying a
growing intervention cost to hold the world there against exogenous drift.
CI$^E$ is designed to measure that maintenance burden directly. In that sense,
performative prediction characterizes where the system settles, whereas the
diagnostic developed here characterizes how much forcing is required to stay
there.

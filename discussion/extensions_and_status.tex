\section{Transfer Results and Operational Questions}\label{sec:extensions_status}

The horizon law extends cleanly to finite projection families and sliced
geometries under uniform projected control. Those results preserve the
finite-sample versus staleness decomposition while changing the metric geometry
and, therefore, the finite-sample exponent.

In fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn geometry, the pointwise and bandwise
one-dimensional inheritance results are proved on compact support. On bounded-
cardinality finite discrete embedded classes with bounded diameter and a uniform
mass floor, the same moderate-band root-$n$ law follows from a finite-state
minorization argument for the corrected $S^2$ self-coupling route together with
finite-dimensional influence control. On the calibrated support-growth family,
the same law follows from the exact $\ell_2$ fluctuation identity, the row-
kernel parity decomposition, and the local block-curvature theorem. Finite-
difference Hessian probes indicate bounded local curvatures and collective
curvatures that scale approximately linearly with the support-growth parameter.
The local-basis trace proxy grows linearly in the support-growth parameter at
about $0.34$ per block, while the covariance-weighted proxy stays small and
stable around $0.002$ to $0.004$.

Operationally, online control begins with horizon inference: the plug-in horizon
must first enter the useful-memory region and then remain there under streaming
noise. The available evidence supports this mechanism on the tested profiles,
but it does not yield a detector-uniform dominance law.

Finite-family transfer and bounded-cardinality finite-discrete Sinkhorn
inheritance are theorem-level, while support-change beyond the fixed support-
growth construction and online control remain open.

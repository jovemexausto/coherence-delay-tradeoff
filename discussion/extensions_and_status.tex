\section{Transfer Results and Operational Questions}\label{sec:extensions_status}

The horizon law extends cleanly to finite projection families and sliced
geometries under uniform projected control. Those results preserve the
finite-sample versus staleness decomposition while changing the metric geometry
and, therefore, the finite-sample exponent.

In fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn geometry, the pointwise and bandwise
one-dimensional inheritance results are proved on compact support. On the
embedded fixed-span model, the moderate-band theorem is conditional on the
centered self-coupling stability lemma and the associated uniform linearization
control. The calibrated $(8,2)$ and $(12,2)$ regimes support that conditional
picture, but they do not resolve it.

Operationally, online control begins with horizon inference: the plug-in horizon
must first enter the useful-memory region and then remain there under streaming
noise. The available evidence supports this mechanism on the tested profiles,
but it does not yield a detector-uniform dominance law.

Finite-family transfer is theorem-level, the embedded Sinkhorn statement is
conditional, and online control remains open.

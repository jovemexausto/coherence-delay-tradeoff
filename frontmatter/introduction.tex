\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Inferential structures possess finite persistence under flow. The temporal-
validity functional
\[
  V(\phi,\tau)
  = \inf_{n\ge 1}\bigl(C_K(\phi)n^{-a(\phi)} + C_S\zeta(\tau)n^{H(\tau)}\bigr)
\]
assigns that persistence value to an inferential structure $\phi$ and a drift
class $\tau$. Here $\phi$ indexes the geometry and estimation rule used to
retain evidence, while $\tau$ indexes the admissible flow of the target law.
Retention lowers sampling noise and ages evidence at the same time. Change
detection exposes the same relation: a detector can remain silent after retained
evidence has already become stale.

The working object is the validity field $V$ on inferential structures and flow.

The $V$-optimal problem asks, for fixed $\tau$, which geometry $\phi$ minimizes
$V(\phi,\tau)$. That geometry-selection problem is distinct from the memory-
length optimization at fixed $(\phi,\tau)$ and becomes visible only after the
functional is written in closed form.

Let $P_t$ denote the present target law, let $\bar P_t^{(n)}$ denote the window
target formed from the last $n$ target laws, and let $\widehat P_t^{(n)}$ be the
estimator built from the last $n$ observations. In the deterministic
H"older--Wasserstein constitutive model, tracking error separates into a
finite-sample term relative to $\bar P_t^{(n)}$ and a staleness term between
$\bar P_t^{(n)}$ and $P_t$:
\[
  \mathbb E\,d\!\left(\widehat P_t^{(n)},P_t\right)
  \le C_K n^{-a} + C_S\zeta n^H.
\]
When the finite-sample term decays like $n^{-a}$ and the staleness term grows
like $\zeta n^H$, their balance defines the temporal-validity horizon and the
induced useful-memory region. In the deterministic H"older--Wasserstein
constitutive model, this balance realizes the temporal-validity functional
\[
  V(\phi,\tau)=\inf_{n\ge 1}\bigl(C_K(\phi)n^{-a(\phi)}+C_S\zeta(\tau)n^{H(\tau)}\bigr).
\]

The finite-sample exponent $a$ depends on the geometry used to compare
distributions, while the roughness exponent $H$ and amplitude $\zeta$ describe
how quickly retained evidence loses temporal validity. The resulting horizon is
structural: it is imposed jointly by metric geometry and drift regularity.

Three proved cases make the law concrete. A one-dimensional root-$n$ proof
anchor closes the basic persistence regime, while bounded-cardinality finite-
discrete and calibrated support-growth embedded Sinkhorn classes show that the
same mechanism persists in operational transport geometry.

The benchmark closure is the Gaussian location minimax theorem on the
deterministic H\"older class, where the upper and lower horizon rates meet.

Finite projection families and sliced transport provide transfer theorems,
validity observability from lag geometry yields identifiability and regret
bounds, and the same object appears empirically through U-curves, roughness
scaling, and validity loss before detector alarm.

Inferential persistence is the central object: the residual validity profile of
an inferential structure relative to a nonstationary flow.

This formulation opens a small program of five frontiers: class-tight lower
theory beyond the Gaussian benchmark, transfer beyond the proved projected
range, support-changing embedded Sinkhorn, online validity control under
streaming noise, and flow models beyond deterministic H"older flow.

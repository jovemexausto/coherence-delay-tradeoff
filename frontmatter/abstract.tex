\begin{abstract}
The temporal-validity profile of retained evidence under drift determines how
long information remains operationally useful. Retaining more data reduces
estimation noise but increases temporal mismatch, so finite memory traces out a
validity geometry rather than a single optimal window. The finite-sample and
drift-induced dissipation terms balance at the temporal-validity horizon,
yielding an optimal memory law and a normalized useful-memory profile. The law
closes in a one-dimensional root-$n$ proof anchor and in embedded Sinkhorn
classes; finite projection and sliced transport transfer the law under uniform
projected control. Lag-geometry inference then yields identifiability and
quadratic regret guarantees for horizon estimation. Experiments exhibit the
predicted U-curves, roughness-dependent scaling, real-stream validity
signatures, and validity loss before detector alarm. The validity-detection gap
also motivates a finite-horizon detector-class sign law under null false-alarm
control.
\end{abstract}

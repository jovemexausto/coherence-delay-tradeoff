\begin{abstract}
We study the temporal validity of retained evidence for finite-memory distribution tracking under drift. Expected tracking error is controlled by a tradeoff between finite-sample accuracy and drift-induced staleness, so that longer memory reduces sampling noise while increasing age mismatch with the present distribution. This balance induces a finite temporal-validity horizon and an optimal memory scale $n^*(a,H)\asymp (C_K/\zeta)^{1/(a+H)}$, where the finite-sample rate exponent $a$ and the temporal roughness exponent $H$ determine how quickly the horizon expands or contracts. We establish this horizon in a tractable one-dimensional proof model, and a Gaussian lower bound shows that the phenomenon is structural rather than estimator-specific. The experiments exhibit the predicted interior memory optimum, roughness-dependent scaling, and a measurable lag between validity loss and the first changepoint alarm. Under drift, retained evidence can stop representing the present before change becomes statistically detectable.

\end{abstract}

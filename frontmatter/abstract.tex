\begin{abstract}
Tracking in a changing environment is governed by a simple tension: more memory
reduces noise, but it also makes the estimate older. In passive streams this is
already a statistical problem; in closed-loop systems it becomes an epistemic
one, because the same actions used to stabilize behavior can also make a stale
model look correct. The result is a two-part problem: a finite-memory tracking
limit, and a masking diagnostic for systems whose actions shape what they later
observe.

The first contribution formalizes a coherence-delay trade-off for finite-memory
tracking under drift. For the finite-memory averaging class studied here,
variance falls with memory while lag grows with drift and effective age,
yielding a \textbf{cube-root law}: the \textbf{minimum achievable tracking
error} scales as $\boldsymbol{\zeta^{1/3}}$ with drift rate $\zeta$, and the
\textbf{optimal effective memory} scales as $\boldsymbol{\zeta^{-2/3}}$.
Empirically, sliding windows and EWMA recover slopes of 0.316 and 0.317,
closely matching the theoretical prediction.

The second contribution concerns the regime in which low observed error is
maintained not by adaptation but by steering the environment toward a stale
model. We call this \emph{coercive masking} and introduce the
\textbf{Coherence Index (CI)} and the \textbf{Effort-Adjusted Coherence Index
($CI^E$)} to separate genuine adaptation from action-induced alignment. On
passive benchmarks, generic changepoint detectors remain the stronger default
alarms. On active synthetic and logged recommendation benchmarks, however,
$CI^E$ exposes masking that passive detectors do not encode under matched
calibration, identifying masking onset with median delay 5.0 steps in the
particle benchmark and improving KuaiRand bubble detection from 0.458 to 0.692
on a proxy-level logged benchmark, with paired user-level bootstrap intervals
excluding zero.

The picture is straightforward. Under drift, finite memory imposes a real error
floor. Under feedback, raw fit can be misleading unless the cost of
intervention is made explicit. The theory explains the floor; the diagnostic
reveals when apparent coherence is being purchased rather than earned.

\end{abstract}

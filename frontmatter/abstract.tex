\begin{abstract}
Finite-memory distribution tracking under drift is governed by a temporal-validity horizon for retained evidence. Expected tracking error satisfies
\[
  \mathbb E\,d\!\left(\widehat P_t^{(n)},P_t\right)
  \leq
  C_K n^{-a} + C_S\zeta n^H,
\]
where $a>0$ is the finite-sample rate exponent and $H\in(0,1]$ is the temporal roughness exponent. Balancing these terms yields the memory scale
\[
  n^*(a,H)\asymp (C_K/\zeta)^{1/(a+H)}.
\]
\newline
The present work identifies this horizon as the scale at which
retained evidence remains statistically current. A tractable one-dimensional
proof model establishes the root-$n$ regime, and a Gaussian lower bound shows
that the horizon is structural rather than estimator-specific. Memory under
drift is therefore governed by temporal validity, and
retained evidence can become invalid for the present before change becomes
statistically detectable.

\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
We study the temporal validity of retained evidence for distribution tracking
under drift. The central object is a temporal-validity horizon: the memory scale
at which the gain from larger samples is overtaken by drift-induced staleness.
A general law balances a finite-sample term and a staleness term, yielding an
optimal memory scale and a useful-memory region whose normalized geometry
depends only on the finite-sample and drift exponents. We prove this law in a
tractable one-dimensional root-$n$ proof anchor, and we prove it again in
theorem-level embedded Sinkhorn classes, including bounded-cardinality finite
discrete classes and a calibrated support-growth family. Finite projection
families and sliced transport extend the same law under uniform projected
control. A horizon-inference
analysis propagates lag-geometry uncertainty into data-driven memory selection
through an identifiability criterion and a quadratic regret law. Experiments
reveal interior U-curves, roughness-dependent scaling, a real-stream finite-
memory signature, and a regime in which temporal validity fails before standard
changepoint alarms.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
We study the temporal validity of retained evidence for distribution tracking
under drift. The central object is a temporal-validity horizon: the memory scale
at which the gain from larger samples is overtaken by drift-induced staleness.
A general law balances a finite-sample term and a staleness term, yielding an
optimal memory scale and a useful-memory region whose normalized geometry
depends only on the finite-sample and drift exponents. We prove this law in a
tractable one-dimensional root-$n$ proof model, show through a Gaussian
lower-bound construction and a Gaussian location benchmark that the horizon is
structural, and extend the horizon law to finite projection families and
one-dimensional fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn geometry. An inferable-horizon
layer propagates lag-geometry uncertainty into data-driven memory selection
through an operational identifiability criterion and a quadratic regret law.
Experiments reveal interior U-curves, roughness-dependent scaling, a real-stream
retention signature, and a regime in which temporal validity fails before
standard changepoint alarms.
\end{abstract}

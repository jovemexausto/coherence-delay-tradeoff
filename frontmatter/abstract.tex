\begin{abstract}
We study tracking under drift as an estimation problem with finite effective
memory. When total tracking error is decomposed into a statistical term and a
drift-induced staleness term, minimizing over window size yields a cube-root
law for the estimator class analyzed here:
$\mathcal{E}_{\min} = \tfrac{3}{2}C_K^{2/3}\zeta^{1/3}$ with optimal window
$n^* = (C_K/\zeta)^{2/3}$. We interpret this trade-off as a limit on online
compression of a non-stationary process: larger memory reduces variance but
extends the temporal mismatch between the model and the current environment.

Action-coupled settings motivate a minimal decomposition into representation,
actuation, and adaptation components, denoted $(\Pot, \Act, \Conv)$. In the
class of action-effective systems studied here, the update depends on the
current internal state, the most recent action, and the incoming signal;
dropping any one of these leaves the drift-coherent class analyzed in the
paper. This decomposition yields a coherence score, $\Score$, together with an
effort-corrected version, $\ScoreE$, for settings where feedback masking can
make stale models look healthy by forcing the environment toward an obsolete
internal estimate.

Empirically, a Gaussian tracker confirms the predicted
U-curve and the $\zeta^{1/3}$ scaling law on synthetic drift processes
(observed slope $0.316$, 95\% CI $[0.287, 0.345]$), while the particle tracker
shows that coercive masking can keep the
standard score artificially high ($0.949 \pm 0.012$) even when the
effort-corrected score falls ($0.697 \pm 0.046$). On the ELEC2 and Bikes
passive benchmarks, ADWIN remains the strongest generic changepoint detector on
the residual streams, as expected for a passive-stream baseline, while the
coherence scores remain an interpretable structural diagnostic. On the KuaiRand
logged benchmark (367 eligible users, healthy-phase calibration), $\ScoreE$
raises bubble detection from 45.8\% to 69.2\% and collapse detection from
54.8\% to 70.0\%.

\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
We investigate the fundamental limits of tracking an evolving distribution under
temporal drift. Longer memory reduces finite-sample estimation noise but also
introduces staleness through age mismatch with the present state. This balance is
formalized by a finite temporal-validity horizon, which induces a near-optimal
useful-memory region around the scale $n^*(a,H) \asymp (C_K/\zeta)^{1/(a+H)}$;
the finite-sample rate exponent $a$ and the temporal roughness exponent $H$
govern the expansion and contraction of that region. Under explicit compact-
support and fixed-span assumptions, a tractable one-dimensional proof model
yields a root-$n$ finite-sample term, with verifiable sufficient conditions for
the integrated Bahadur remainder on a smooth deformation class. A Gaussian lower
bound shows that the boundary is structural rather than estimator-specific, and
the corresponding Gaussian location model admits a minimax benchmark theorem on
deterministic H\"older paths. Beyond one dimension, sliced or coordinate-
averaged Wasserstein gives a multivariate root-$n$ transfer route under uniform
projected control. Finally, empirical evidence confirms the predicted interior
memory optima, roughness-dependent scaling, the same retention signature on a
real stream, and a measurable lag between the loss of temporal validity and the
activation of standard changepoint alarms.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
We investigate the fundamental limits of tracking an evolving distribution under
temporal drift. Longer memory reduces finite-sample estimation noise but also
introduces staleness through age mismatch with the present state. This balance is
formalized by a finite temporal-validity horizon, which induces a near-optimal
useful-memory region around the scale $n^*(a,H) \asymp (C_K/\zeta)^{1/(a+H)}$;
the finite-sample rate exponent $a$ and the temporal roughness exponent $H$
govern the expansion and contraction of that region. These theoretical limits are
established within a tractable one-dimensional framework, with a Gaussian lower
bound showing that the boundary is structural rather than estimator-specific.
Finally, empirical evidence confirms the predicted interior memory optima,
roughness-dependent scaling, the same retention signature on a real stream, and
a measurable lag between the loss of temporal validity and the activation of
standard changepoint alarms.
\end{abstract}

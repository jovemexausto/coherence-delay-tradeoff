\begin{abstract}
The object of study is validity of an information state under drift, with
temporal validity as one distinguished slice. An information state may be a
windowed empirical law, a transport estimator, a calibration set, or a
controller memory, but in every case retained data reduce finite-sample noise
while accumulating mismatch with the present. For an information-state pipeline
$\phi$ and a flow family $\tau$, the validity functional $V(\phi,\tau)$ records
the best achievable present-time error over memory scale. In the deterministic
H"older--Wasserstein model, this yields the constitutive law
\[
  \mathbb E\,d\!\left(\widehat P_t^{(n)},P_t\right)
  \le C_K n^{-a} + C_S\zeta n^H,
\]
with horizon scale $n^*(a,H)\asymp (C_K/\zeta)^{1/(a+H)}$ and a nontrivial
useful-memory band. A Gaussian location benchmark closes the exponent of the
law, and a tractable one-dimensional proof model together with projection and
sliced transfer establish the same mechanism beyond the benchmark class.
Lag-geometry observability yields plug-in inference and operational
identifiability for the horizon. Empirically, the predicted U-curves,
roughness-dependent scaling, validity-before-detection gap, and sequential
phase map all appear in synthetic and real streams. A split-conformal benchmark
under drift exhibits the same geometry: the calibration window has an interior
optimum, coverage degrades before alarm, and the empirically safe calibration
band coincides with the useful-memory band. Validity is therefore a structural
property of retained information itself, with temporal validity and conformal
calibration as operational readouts.
\end{abstract}

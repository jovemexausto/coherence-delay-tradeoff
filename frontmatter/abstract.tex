\begin{abstract}
The temporal-validity functional $V(\phi,\tau)$ assigns a persistence value to
an inferential structure $\phi$ against a drift class $\tau$: it is the minimum
achievable tracking error at the optimal memory scale, and the best retained
evidence can do before flow makes it stale. Here $\phi$ ranges over inferential
structures and $\tau$ over admissible flow classes, so $V$ is a persistence
field over structure and flow. In the deterministic H"older--Wasserstein
constitutive model, the finite-sample and drift-induced dissipation terms
balance at the temporal-validity horizon, yielding an optimal memory law and a normalized useful-memory profile. The benchmark closure is the
Gaussian location minimax theorem on the deterministic H\"older class, where
the upper and lower horizon rates meet. A one-dimensional root-$n$ proof anchor
and embedded Sinkhorn classes establish nontrivial instances of the same
geometry, while finite projection and sliced transport transfer the law under
uniform projected control. Validity observability from lag geometry yields identifiability and
quadratic regret guarantees for horizon estimation. Experiments exhibit the
predicted U-curves, roughness-dependent scaling, real-stream validity
signatures, and validity loss before detector alarm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Let $\{P_t\}_{t \geq 0}$ be a family of probability measures on
$\R^d$ evolving over time.  The object of interest is the lagged transport
geometry encoded by the Wasserstein-2 discrepancy curve
\[
    D_j \;:=\; W_2(P_{t-j}, P_t),\qquad j = 1, 2, \ldots, L.
\]
This curve is typically observed through estimators $\Dhj$ constructed from
$n$ i.i.d.\ samples from each $P_{t-j}$ and $P_t$, rather than through the
population values $D_j$ themselves.  Under standard conditions on the measures
\citep{FournierGuillin2015,GoldfeldEtAl2022,delBarrioEtAl2023}, the plug-in Wasserstein estimator
satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \Dhj = D_j + \varepsilon_j,\qquad
    \varepsilon_j \sim \mathrm{AN}\!\left(0,\; \frac{\sigma_0^2}{n}\right),
    \label{eq:obs-model}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_0^2 > 0$ is a nuisance constant depending on the local
geometry of the measures.

A low-dimensional structural hypothesis is that the discrepancy curve follows
a \emph{power law}:
\begin{equation}
    D_j = \zeta\,j^H,\qquad \zeta > 0,\; H \in (0,1).
    \label{eq:power-law}
\end{equation}
This compresses the lag curve to the two-parameter family $(\zeta, H)$, with
exponent $H$ interpreted as a roughness index of
the latent drift in locally stationary settings \citep{Dahlhaus1997,Vogt2012,TinioAlayaBouzebda2025}.

The inferential problem is not standard.  In log-space,
$\Var(\log \Dhj) \approx \sigma_0^2/(n D_j^2) = \sigma_0^2 /(n\,\zeta^2\,j^{2H})$,
so the observation noise depends on the same scale law that is being
inferred.  The family is low-dimensional, but the noise is
parameter-coupled.

This coupling induces a single information scale across the paper.  With
oracle weights $w_j^* = nD_j^2/\sigma_0^2$, the effective lag-information is
\[
    \mathcal I_{n,L}(H)
    \;:=\;
    n \sum_{j=1}^L j^{2H},
\]
up to constants depending on $\zeta$ and $\sigma_0$, or more precisely the
matrix information $\mathbf X^\top \mathbf W^* \mathbf X$.  This quantity
drives the variance of $\widehat H$, the departure scale of the residual test,
and the minimax boundary for distinguishing the power-law family from
departure alternatives.  The regimes $L$ fixed and $L \to \infty$
are therefore two specialisations of the same law: for fixed $L$,
$\mathcal I_{n,L}(H) \asymp n$, whereas for consecutive lags
$1,\dots,L_n$ with $L_n \to \infty$,
$\mathcal I_{n,L_n}(H) \asymp nL_n^{2H+1}$.

The main results establish a complete inference layer for this family: a
feasible WLS limit theorem for $H$, exact oracle calibration of a residual
adequacy statistic, a minimax lower bound for distinguishing the family from
departure alternatives, and adaptivity in $H$.

\paragraph{Main contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Feasible inference for the exponent.}  \Cref{thm:clt-fwls}
gives a central limit theorem for the two-step feasible WLS estimator
$\Hhat$ of $H$ in the heteroscedastic log-scale model.  The
pilot-estimation error does not inflate the asymptotic variance beyond the
oracle rate.

\item \textbf{Residual adequacy test.}  \Cref{thm:null-chi2} shows that
the weighted residual sum of squares $Q$ satisfies $Q \sim \chi^2(L-2)$
under the power-law family with oracle weights, and that two-step FWLS
converges to the same law asymptotically as $n \to \infty$ with $L$ fixed.

\item \textbf{Minimax boundary for model distinguishability.}
\Cref{thm:lecam-lower} gives the minimax separation boundary
$\kappa^* \asymp \mathcal I_{n,L}(H)^{-1/2}$, equivalently
$\bigl(n\sum_{j \leq L} j^{2H}\bigr)^{-1/2}$, for distinguishing the
power-law family from alternatives with departure level
$\kappa \geq \kappa^*$.

\item \textbf{Adaptive structural inference.}  \Cref{thm:adaptivity}
shows that the FWLS adequacy test achieves the minimax rate without
prior knowledge of $H$.

\item \textbf{Simulation evidence.}  The numerical study reports exact
size calibration, FWLS-oracle agreement, and power curves consistent with the
boundary $\kappa^* \asymp \mathcal I_{n,L}(H)^{-1/2}$ (\Cref{sec:sims}).
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the observation model and the information
scale. Section~\ref{sec:clt} gives the feasible inference theorem. Section~
\ref{sec:test} establishes the residual adequacy law, Section~\ref{sec:lower}
proves the separation lower bound, and Section~\ref{sec:adaptivity} extends
the boundary to the growing-lag regime. Sections~\ref{sec:sims} and
\ref{sec:application} provide the empirical signatures and the operational
implication, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} closes the program.

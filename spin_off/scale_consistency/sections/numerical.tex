\section{Simulation Evidence}
\label{sec:sims}

All experiments use the simulation model \eqref{eq:M} with $\zeta = 1$,
$\sigma_0 = 1$, $H = 0.6$ (unless stated), and the two-step FWLS procedure.
We used 8000 Monte Carlo replications for size and 5000 for power; detailed
numerical tables are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:numerical_tables}.

\subsection{Null Calibration}

Figure~\ref{fig:null-calibration} shows that the residual adequacy statistic is
well calibrated at moderate lag counts: its empirical mean tracks the oracle
null mean closely, and the rejection frequency remains near the nominal
level. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:null} gives the exact grid values.

\subsection{Power at the Information-Scale Separation Boundary}

Figure~\ref{fig:power-boundary} shows the transition at the information-scale
boundary $\kappa^* \asymp \bigl(n\sum_{j \leq L} j^{2H}\bigr)^{-1/2}$:
the adequacy test is informative but not overconfident at $c=1$, and it gains
power steadily above the boundary.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]

\subsection{Feasible-Oracle Agreement}

Figure~\ref{fig:fwls-oracle-gap} shows that the feasible estimator tracks the
oracle benchmark closely: the RMSE ratio stays numerically at one, while the
gap shrinks with $n$. This is the numerical counterpart of the CLT. Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:fwls-oracle} reports the corresponding grid values.

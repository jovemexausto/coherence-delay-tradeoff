\section{Unknown-Scale Extension}
\label{sec:application}

Unknown $\sigma_0$ introduces self-normalization into the adequacy statistic.
The clean benchmark is obtained by decoupling scale estimation from residual
testing. Let block $A$ and block $B$ be independent copies of the Gaussian
observation model under $H_0$, with effective sample sizes $n_A$ and $n_B$.
Write $x_j = \log j$ and $\alpha = \log \zeta$.

\begin{proposition}[Oracle Split-$F$ Benchmark]
\label{prop:oracle-split-f}
Assume that the power-law profile $(\alpha,H)$ is known and define the scale
estimator on block $A$ by
\[
    \hat\sigma_{A}^{2}
    :=
    \frac{n_A}{L}
    \sum_{j=1}^{L} D_j^2\bigl(Y^{A}_{j} - \alpha - Hx_j\bigr)^2.
\]
On block $B$, form the oracle weighted residual statistic
\[
    Q_{B,\mathrm{split}}^*
    :=
    \sum_{j=1}^{L} \hat w^{(A)}_j \bigl(U^{*(B)}_j\bigr)^2,
    \qquad
    \hat w^{(A)}_j = \frac{n_B D_j^2}{\hat\sigma_A^2},
\]
where $U^{*(B)}_j$ are the oracle WLS residuals computed on block $B$.
Then
\[
    Q_{B,\mathrm{split}}^* \sim (L-2)\,F_{L-2,L}
\]
exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under $H_0$,
\[
    \frac{n_A}{\sigma_0^2}
    \sum_{j=1}^{L} D_j^2\bigl(Y^{A}_{j} - \alpha - Hx_j\bigr)^2
    \sim \chi^2_L,
\]
because the block-$A$ coordinates are independent centered Gaussians with known
mean and variance $\sigma_0^2/(n_A D_j^2)$. On block $B$, oracle WLS projects
onto a two-dimensional regression space, so
\[
    \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2} Q_{B,\mathrm{oracle}}^* \sim \chi^2_{L-2}
\]
with independence from block $A$. Since
\[
    Q_{B,\mathrm{split}}^*
    =
    \frac{\sigma_0^2}{\hat\sigma_A^2} Q_{B,\mathrm{oracle}}^*
    =
    \frac{L\,\chi^2_{L-2}}{\chi^2_L},
\]
the stated $F$ law follows.
\end{proof}

The benchmark law isolates the decoupled geometry of the unknown-scale problem.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:sigma0-plugin} then separates three feasible behaviors
on the tested grid. Naive same-sample $\chi^2$ calibration collapses to
near-zero rejection. The fully feasible split-$F$ procedure is conservative.
Same-sample parametric bootstrap restores rejection frequencies close to the
nominal level. The theorem-level statement therefore belongs to the decoupled
benchmark, while the bootstrap path provides the operational extension.

\section{Empirical Signatures}\label{sec:validation}

The empirical section makes the theory visible in finite samples. The aim is
to exhibit the main observable signatures of the temporal-validity horizon and
to report evidence relevant to the fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn conjecture.

All experiments in this section are synthetic and fully specified by the code in
the repository. The Gaussian U-curve uses 20 Monte Carlo seeds; the
roughness-family sweep uses 12 seeds across the stated H\"older envelopes; the
fixed-$\varepsilon$ operational map is calibrated from log--log finite-sample
fits over sample sizes $(24,48,96,160)$ on the ambient/intrinsic pairs $(8,1)$,
$(8,2)$, $(12,1)$, and $(12,2)$ using 24 seeds; and the validity-detection
experiment uses a piecewise-ramped drift stream with 12 seeds, a fixed
operating window, and both ADWIN and Page--Hinkley detectors. In the
operational map, a cell is marked useful when both fitted finite-sample
exponents stay above the usefulness threshold and their gap remains below the
calibration tolerance. Reported curves are seed-averaged means, while the map
summarizes log--log slope fits across the listed sample sizes. The empirical
goal is qualitative and structural: persistence of shape, ordering, and sign
across repeated runs, not precise constant estimation for every curve.

Four signatures matter. First, finite-memory risk should exhibit an interior
optimum. Second, that optimum should move with temporal roughness. Third, the
fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn conjecture should receive structured empirical
support rather than a single undifferentiated claim. Fourth, temporal validity
can fail before changepoint evidence becomes statistically visible.

\subsection{A Finite-Memory Optimum}\label{sec:validation_ucurve}

The Gaussian horizon sweep isolates the variance--staleness trade-off directly.
Small windows are variance dominated, large windows are stale, and the minimum
lies at an interior horizon.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\safeincludegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{artifacts/figures/gaussian/fig_ucurve}
\caption{Finite-memory U-curve. Tracking error first falls with horizon and then
rises once staleness overtakes the remaining variance gain. The optimum remains
finite across drift strengths.}
\label{fig:ucurve}
\end{figure}

This is the basic empirical signature of temporal validity under drift. Under
stationarity, more memory would continue to help. Under drift, the same horizon
that reduces variance also ages the evidence.

\subsection{Roughness-Dependent Horizon Scaling}
\label{sec:validation_roughness}

The next experiment varies the roughness index directly. For each H\"older-type
regime, the surrogate error obeys the same structural decomposition as the
theory,
\[
  \mathcal E(n) \approx \sigma n^{-1/2} + \zeta n^H,
\]
and the experiment records the empirically optimal horizon across a sweep of
$\zeta$ values.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\safeincludegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{artifacts/figures/roughness_family/fig_roughness_family}
\caption{Roughness-dependent horizon scaling. Each path class exhibits a finite
optimum, and the location of that optimum changes with $H$. The horizon is a
function of drift regularity, not a fixed window rule.}
\label{fig:roughness_family}
\end{figure}

The empirical optima separate by roughness class, matching the path-dependent
horizon logic of \cref{cor:optimized_horizon}.

\subsection{Evidence for the Sinkhorn Conjecture}
\label{sec:validation_operational_frontier}

The fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn experiments address the bandwise question: when
a practical geometry is used in place of raw $W_2$, does the finite-sample term
remain stable enough for the conjectured horizon law to stay informative? The
pointwise one-dimensional fixed-support statement appears in
Proposition~\ref{prop:pointwise_sinkhorn_1d}; the calibrated embedded fixed-span
model shows the regime map.

The figure below concerns fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn geometry on embedded
low-intrinsic-dimensional support. It shows that the corresponding finite-sample
term remains stable across the tested regularization values and that the
triangular slope stays close to the i.i.d. benchmark rather than exhibiting a
separate exponent. Combined with the structural ingredients isolated in
\cref{prop:operational_theorem_frontier}, this provides empirical support for
the bandwise conjecture in \cref{conj:operational_sinkhorn_horizon}. In the
calibrated fixed-span model with $\alpha=2$ and span $0.25$, the maximal
observed stable bands are $\varepsilon_{\max}=0.5$ for $(d,k)=(8,1)$,
$(8,2)$, and $(12,2)$, and $\varepsilon_{\max}=0.2$ for $(12,1)$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\linewidth]{artifacts/figures/frontier/fig_operational_frontier_map.pdf}
\caption{Empirical shape relevant to the bandwise fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn
conjecture. Each panel reports the minimum of the i.i.d. and triangular
finite-sample exponents over the ambient/intrinsic pairs tested at one
regularization level. Crossed cells mark settings that fail the usefulness
criterion. The resulting picture has a stable core and a small mixed boundary
region on the tested grid.}
\label{fig:operational_frontier_map}
\end{figure}

This map gives the shape of the empirical support behind the bandwise conjecture.
The observed operational region is neither empty nor uniform across settings; the
calibration details and supporting tables are kept in
\cref{app:finite_sample_geometry}. The tested grid exhibits a stable operational
core, and the bandwise inheritance law is conjectural outside the calibrated
fixed-span settings shown here.

To probe whether the frontier is controlled more by local intrinsic geometry than
by the ambient coordinate count, the repository also computes a TwoNN-based
estimate of the effective intrinsic dimension on the same synthetic frontier
family. On the tested grid, that estimate predicts the stable-band cutoff
better than ambient dimension and improves the binary cut at \(\varepsilon=0.2\),
while the rowwise stability pattern remains mixed; the point is therefore
regime selection by estimated local geometry, not a universal replacement for
the theorem-level band analysis \citep{FaccoEtAl2017}.

\subsection{Validity Loss Before Detector Alarm}\label{sec:validation_invalidity_gap}

Temporal validity and changepoint evidence are different clocks. To make that
separation explicit, consider a stream whose local drift rate ramps upward over
time. The horizon $n_t^*$ contracts as the ramp steepens, while a long
operating horizon remains fixed. Define
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{val-det}} \coloneqq t_{\mathrm{detect}} - t_{\mathrm{valid}},
\]
where $t_{\mathrm{valid}}$ is the first time the operating horizon persistently
exceeds the temporal-validity horizon, and $t_{\mathrm{detect}}$ is the first
detector alarm that follows.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\safeincludegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{artifacts/figures/invalidity_gap/fig_invalidity_gap}
\caption{Validity loss before detector alarm. As drift intensifies, the
temporal-validity horizon contracts until the operating horizon becomes stale at
$t_{\mathrm{valid}}$; the detector responds only later at $t_{\mathrm{detect}}$,
leaving a strictly positive validity-detection lag. The lower panel shows
excess tracking error accumulating before the alarm appears.}
\label{fig:invalidity_gap}
\end{figure}

Across the reported runs, the measured lag remains positive. Retained evidence
can already be invalid for the present while detector statistics remain below
alarm threshold.

The positive lag is not a knife-edge feature of one tuning choice. Across the
tested ADWIN thresholds, mean detection time remains strictly later than mean
validity-expiry time, and the same qualitative lag persists under a different
detector family as well. The empirical claim is robustness of the sign of
$\Delta_{\mathrm{val-det}}$ on the tested detector/tuning family, not a
detector-uniform delay law.

\subsection{What the Evidence Shows}\label{sec:validation_summary}

Taken together, the experiments support four claims that match the theory.
Finite-memory tracking exhibits a structural U-curve rather than an ever-growing
memory benefit. The temporal-validity horizon is roughness dependent. The
fixed-$\varepsilon$ Sinkhorn conjecture receives structured empirical support on
the tested grid. Temporal validity can fail before changepoint evidence appears,
producing a measurable validity-detection lag.
